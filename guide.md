## Program Overview

The New York Family Offense Petition Program is a free, interactive document assembly tool for advocates and legal service providers to quickly and easily create court papers required to request a temporary order of protection. The program is led by the New York State Unified Court System in partnership with Pro Bono Net.

### Background from Lisa Smith

Lisa Smith is part of the New York State Office of Court Administration, Division of Policy and Planning, led by Deputy Administrative Judge Edwina Mendelsohn. She thanked her colleague Amelia Laporte from the Division of Technology and partners Eugenie Ortiz and Claudia Johnson.

The program allows trained advocates to assist domestic violence survivors in creating a family offense petition from a remote location. Previously, survivors had to go in person to the local family court to fill out the petition. The program also allows requesting a remote ex parte hearing in family court, at which the judge hears evidence and may issue a full or limited order of protection.

- Approximately 220 advocacy organizations are approved to use the program.
- It is available in all 62 counties.
- It has been especially important during COVID, but was conceived several years earlier for survivors who are physically challenged, in rural locations where transportation is an issue, or have child care problems — anyone for whom a trip to family court was problematic.

### Remote Petition Guide

The Remote Petition Guide handout is very important. On page two, step-by-step instructions for starting are provided:

1. **Contact your local family court** — A list of contacts is provided so you have someone to reach out to if there is a problem. Let them know you are planning to participate in the program.
2. **Have your agency added to the program** — Your agency must be approved. Page two gives exact instructions on how to be approved.
3. **Training** — Once approved, your agency will need to be trained. An email address on page two will alert Lisa that you are an approved agency, and she will coordinate the training.

## LawHelp Interactive (LHI) Platform

Claudia Johnson explained that there are two levels of sign-up to use the program:

1. **Pro Bono Net page** — Access to information related to the program, list of court contacts, manual, etc.
2. **LawHelp Interactive (LHI)** — An independent platform and form engine used in New York and 40 other states with many courts. It creates forms that are fast, secure, reliable, and available 24/7.

### Creating an LHI Account

- If you are a new organization and have never used LHI, you must request access to an organization first, then create your account and tie it to your organization.
- Approximately 220 New York organizations already use LHI, and new ones can be added easily.
- The petition is not open to the public. Users must get an account — specifically an **advocate account**, which does not necessarily require being a lawyer. It is used by all sorts of professionals in different contexts.

### Account Creation Steps

1. Click **"Professional."**
2. Create your account.
3. Select **"Advocate"** account.
4. Select **"New York State."**
5. Enter your organization. If it is not found, contact support and it will be added within a day.

### Before You Begin

The first questions are:

- Did you get approval from your court?
- Have you been in touch with Lisa?
- Do you have access to the Pro Bono Net page?

If not, follow the manual and read page two.

## Live Demonstration of the Interview

Claudia logged into her LHI account and demonstrated the interview process.

### Logging In and Starting

- The system shows where you are, where the interview can be used, when it was last updated, and who owns it — confirming you are in the right place.
- If you have no saved answers, press **"Continue"** to start using the interview.
- The petition may be completed over more than one sitting if additional information is needed.
- Saved answers appear on the account. Click to resume, or click **"Continue"** to create a new petition.

### Navigation

- An **outline** is visible on the screen.
- At the bottom are navigation buttons for moving back and forth.
- **Do not use browser back arrows** — they may ruin your work.
- On mobile, scroll down to make sure navigation buttons are visible.

### County and Organization Selection

- Selecting a county (e.g., Bronx) changes the list of organizations dynamically based on backend logic.
- The interview changes depending on the choices made.

### Required Answers

- Anything in **red** is a required answer. The document will not create without it.
- Example: A firearms question — if you click "Yes," it gives red language indicating a required answer; if you pick "No," the question is not asked. The logic guides you so you do not miss something.

### Offenses Committed

- Check the boxes for the offenses committed.
- An open text box is available to detail what you want to put in.
- The outline shows your progress along the path.

### Editing and Expanding Questions

- If you learn new information (e.g., guns in the home), you can go back and edit.
- The interview expands with relevant questions based on your choices.
- Required questions are marked with a **red asterisk** and the outline expands.
- If a required question is missed, the system flags it.
- You can jump directly to a section (e.g., firearms) using the outline to save time.

### Finishing and Saving

- Once you reach the end, the **Finish** button appears.
- The system processes and puts the data together.
- **Documents are never saved here** — only your answers are saved encrypted.
- After creating the document, a page appears with instructions and a warning: **"Important: Your answers have not been saved. We recommend that you save."**

### How to Save

- Click the **floppy disk icon** in the interview to save.
- The system does **not** save as you go — you must save manually.
- Recommendation: Save as you go and save before you get to finish.
- You can save as **saved answers** or **save as new** (creating two sets of answers).
- When saved, a message confirms your answers are saved.
- Saving is important if you need to come back and edit.

### Downloading and E-Filing

- You can email forms to an advocate or family member, or e-file your form.
- **Never use the browser back button** — use the provided navigation.
- Click **Download** to pull the document. It opens with pre-formatted instructions and the document.
- **Best practice:** Use Microsoft Word on your device. On a cell phone or tablet, ensure you have some word processing, or the document may be misformatted.
- The petition is customized to the county selected (e.g., Bronx address and phone number).
- A note to the clerk indicates the interview was created with this system and who the parties are.
- A statutory required pleading is filled out based on entered data.
- One interview can support as many facts as needed — everything entered will be in the document.
- Be careful saving on a local drive, especially on a public computer.

### Reviewing and Editing

- Review the document. If changes are needed, come back and edit.
- Repeat until satisfied.

### E-Filing Process

- Click the **E-Filing** button.
- A message asks permission and informs you that you are sending data outside the system to the courts.
- Instructions are provided, and you consent to send it to the courts.
- Click the **E-File** button.
- The data is sent directly to the **UCMS** (case management system) and tagged for the county (e.g., Bronx).
- It is a secure data-to-data transmission between the two systems.

### If You Forgot Something

- Press **Cancel** — never the back arrow — to go back and edit your answers.
- You can change any part, such as adding an address where you want to be protected or requesting protection at work.

## Remote Hearing Request Demonstration

Lisa asked Claudia to demonstrate the remote hearing section.

### Petitioner Information

- Request for interpreter (a nice feature).
- Contact information and address.
- Introduction section (may have been skipped).

### Generating Affidavit Support

- Asked: "Would you like to generate an affidavit support for your family? Is the petitioner elderly?"
- Selecting "Yes" triggers more mandatory questions.

### Remote Hearing Details

- **First choice:** Time (hours, minutes, AM/PM) and date (e.g., available on the 23rd).
- **Second choice:** Time and date (e.g., 2 PM).
- **Where petitioner is homebound:** Choose where to pick it up — the family court will scan the temporary order of protection to the filing agency.
- **Service of temporary order:** Sheriff.
- **Name of person helping:** Probably you or someone on your staff.
- **Alternate contacts:** In case they cannot reach the person who did it, they may reach you.

### Electronic Signature Question

- Q: Is the petition electronically signed by the petitioner?
- A: No. This is why the remote hearing section is important.

### How the Affirmation Works

- If a remote hearing is being requested, the petitioner is prompted at the end of the interview to check that they understand the petition is signed under penalty of perjury — it is an **affirmation**.
- By checking the box, the petitioner provides that affirmation, which **substitutes for the actual petition signature**.
- This is because most advocates do not have a way to get an actual signature from the petitioner when doing a remote petition and requesting a remote hearing.
- At the remote hearing, the judge can swear in the petitioner and ask them to affirm the substance of the petition.
- Since petitions are filed electronically, it is important that petitioners verify their identity and swear to the allegations on the court record. The judge can do this at the first ex parte temporary order of protection request appearance.
- The guide includes a section showing what the affirmation looks like.

### If No Remote Hearing Is Requested

- People can use the petition and file it without requesting a remote hearing.
- The advocate proceeds by clicking to finish, producing a document the litigant can sign.
- If the litigant will get the papers and go to court, they can sign the papers traditionally.
- If the litigant is not going to do that, they can check the affirmation, which substitutes for the signature.
- Some courts have had issues with this; a memo was sent to everyone at OCA explaining it. If there is an issue, let Lisa know.
- If no remote hearing is requested, the affirmation will **not** pop up in the petition — it will have to be signed.
- If a remote hearing is requested, the affirmation **will** pop up in the petition program.

### Court-Side Process (Amelia)

- The clerk gets the answer set with all data elements entered on the petition, which helps with data entry — they do not have to retype everything.
- They also get the PDF of the petition, which will either have:
  - A signature line for the litigant to fill out (if not submitting for an immediate remote appearance), or
  - The petition with the affirmation (if remote appearance is requested).
- The court will see either way whether the affirmation was done for the remote appearance or if the litigant will come to court later with a signed copy.
- The guide recommends calling or emailing the court (depending on court preference) to let them know a remote hearing will be requested, giving them a heads-up.

## Q&A Session

### Respondent's Phone Number

**Q:** There is no spot for the respondent's phone number. Can that be modified?

**A:** Yes, it can be brought up. Modifications require adding it to the interview and to the XML answers set received electronically. Until that work is done, enter it in the narrative field so the court gets it on the PDF of the petition. It will be added to the list of requested enhancements.

### Narrative Preparation

**Q:** When using this platform, do we need to have the narrative written down as well?

**A:** It may help to know what you want to type in the open boxes, but it varies depending on whether you are in a confidential place. Think about where you are meeting with the person you are helping — is it an open space or a waiting room? The more prepared people are, the quicker and more confidentially the documents can be sent. By the time you sit down to do this, you have probably had a couple of conversations and may be ready to have it written down or not.

**Warning:** Do not copy and paste. If you have text in a document, do not copy and paste it into the open box — it could bring a character that is not visible but the computer may see it, potentially glitching the transmission.

**On offenses:** Do not worry too much about checking off every type of crime listed. Do your best. This is just a petition; there will be a hearing. If some offenses are left off, the judge presiding over the hearing will make a determination about exactly what offenses were committed. Do not get overwhelmed by looking each up in the penal law and being exactly right about the elements — it is not an issue.

**Note:** This is just about the first ex parte appearance. It is not about the continuation of virtual hearings in family court after the first appearance. That is between the litigants and the judge as they go forward.

### Notarized Verification

**Q:** Is there no notarized verification section at the end?

**A:** Not if you are submitting with a request for a remote hearing. The affirmation is all that you check off.

### Remote Hearing Grant Rate

**Q:** How often do remote hearings get granted?

**A:** Under current circumstances, everything has been remote in different judicial districts for a long time. Initially, when this came out, there was more reluctance about remote hearings, and judges were much more reluctant. That has largely gone by the wayside during COVID. People are encouraged to chime in if that is incorrect. Technical support is available through the help button at the bottom of the system. Some advocates have reported getting calls from judges to do hearings the same day — very fast from when they submit. It depends on the court.

### Amended Petitions

**Q:** Once you e-file a petition and then go back in and edit, does it get refiled as an amended petition automatically?

**A:** When the system was built in 2012, this was discussed. There is a check that sees if there has already been an e-filing and asks if you are sure you want to submit, looking at a number of days. There may be a check on the e-filing piece. It is not known if clerks look at it and contact the person if there is already a petition file. On the court side (Amelia): If you are looking for a same-day remote hearing, clerks generally process petition submissions pretty quickly. If you submit one and realize right away you want to make a change, you can submit it again and let the court know to take your later submission. Clerks see a timestamp when reviewing what has come over from the electronic petition interview, so they can tell which one is the later, corrected one. If you do not make it in time, at the first appearance the judge might direct the litigants to submit an amended petition, or the clerk might handle it case by case. If you notice something you missed, give the court a heads-up — they may say to send it over again because they have not processed it yet.

### Confidential Address

**Q:** Is there an option for keeping petitioner's address confidential?

**A:** Yes. There should be an indicator on the petitioner information where you let us know if that information is confidential. You can also request an address confidentiality application.

### County-Specific Remote Request

**Q:** The remote request part may be specific to each respective county. In Nassau County, the questions do not ask about telephone contact.

**A:** Customizations can be made at the court's request per location. The advocate should reach out to their court contact, who can reach out to Amelia at the Division of Technology to work on implementing changes. If a county does not offer a particular option, it will not be listed.

### Client's Phone Number in Narrative

**Q:** If you add the client's phone number in the narrative section, will that information be visible to the offender at any time?

**A:** They would get a copy of the petition, so yes, that would be available.

### Initial Appearance Section

**Q:** For the initial appearance section, is it just for the elderly or disability, or are we checking it off when requesting a remote hearing?

**A:** For remote hearings, it is for anybody who has a reason to request a remote hearing — anyone who is physically challenged, has a specific hardship about appearing in court (e.g., child care issues, rural area transportation issues), or COVID-related issues. It is not just for the person who is homebound or elderly.

### Supporting Documentation

**Q:** Can you attach supporting documentation to the petition?

**A:** No, not at this time. However, if you are doing a remote hearing, at the appearance itself there will be the ability to submit PDFs or show a picture or other supporting documentation at the electronic virtual appearance.

### Confidential Phone Number

**Q:** Is there a way to opt to keep the petitioner's number confidential like you would with their address?

**A:** Yes, you can include that request. Lisa noted that additional questions in that area may require a one-pager refresher on the details of that process, which will be provided and everyone updated. On assembly: The phone number is put in the cover letter to the clerk but is not included in the petition, so the opposing party does not get the phone number of the person asking to be protected. The phone number is not a mandatory field on the promulgated petition form. The only way it would be in the petition is if you put it in one of the free text fields.

### Reporting

**Q:** Is there a way to run reports by or for an agency, for example, petitions filed by month by county?

**A:** Not addressed in detail.

### Glitches with UCMS

**Q:** There seem to be some glitches when the program interfaces with UCMS. Who should be notified?

**A:** Email Amelia with details, and she will take a look.

### First Person vs. Third Person

**Q:** Should the incidents be described in first person or in third person?

**A:** The incidents should be described in first person.

### Slides Availability

**Q:** Will slides be made available?

**A:** Yes. In the handout section, you can download a copy of the slide deck covering what was covered today. A copy will also be posted to probono.net/ny/family.

### Technical Support

Claudia flagged the help capacity, which is mostly for technical help. For questions about how to contact the clerk or the process for your county, go back to **probono.net/ny/family**, get the list of your county contact, and talk to them. The assembling engine does not know that information.

For technical issues — form not assembling, password issues, or problems with the actual creation of the document — email through the help button. Because there are over 5,800 forms in 40 states, the support desk does not know who you are or what you are using, as confidentiality is taken seriously. When sending a report, include:

- I am in New York.
- I am using the DV petition.
- I am on this sort of browser.

Browser behavior sometimes changes how the system behaves.

**Recommendations:**

- If doing clinics or even one interview, do not use a hotspot or a cell phone where bars go up and down. The system requires a really strong, constant connection. If the bars fluctuate, it could interrupt your working session, and you may not be able to create the document. It could take 20 hours to find that one session and see what happened, because there are about 3,000 people on all the time working on these things.
- Make sure you are on a strong connection that does not have variability at the local level.
- Do not click the back button on the browser — it really discombobulates things. Use the navigation in the system.
- Save often and as you go. If on a hotspot and bars go down, data will be there up to the point where you save. You may lose one or two screens but not everything.
- Preferably work on a laptop that has Microsoft Word.
- Save as you go.
- If you get stuck or something happens, contact support and they will try to work with you to see what happened.

## Future Plans

**Q:** Will other family offense petitions be available this way in the future?

**A:** Right now, only the original family offense petition is available. In the future, adding more could be considered. There are no firm plans right now, but there is always a look to expand and improve upon things that are working well. This process is being used a lot across the courts and has been very helpful — before COVID and now with virtual appearances becoming more prevalent in family courts. It is certainly here to stay, and this will be kept in mind going forward.

**Reminder:** It is very important that you are also signed up for the remote option if you want your clients to be able to have a remote hearing, as opposed to having to walk into the family court for the hearing. If you are not sure if you have the remote option, email Lisa or Amelia to check. Most people do have it now. For new agencies being added, it is being built in as they go. If you were added recently, it is likely you have it, but it can be checked and easily changed if missing.

**Q:** Is this program available for all counties in New York State?

**A:** Yes.
